# The Terminus

*A blackboard on gravitational collapse: what the singularity theorems say, what the
Oppenheimer–Snyder endpoint actually is, and where the standard framing hides an
answerable question inside an unanswerable one.*

Geometric units `G = c = 1`. Throughout, **`s`** is the proper time *remaining* along an
infalling worldline before its terminus, so every asymptotic statement is a limit `s → 0⁺`.
Every number and exponent below is regenerated by `verify.py` (25/25 checks).

---

## 0. Ground rules

Two failure modes are being deliberately avoided.

**Premature synthesis** — the reflex that ends every discussion of singularities with
"and then quantum gravity takes over." That sentence is a coupon for a theory nobody has,
and it is spent long before the classical theory has been exhausted. Classical GR has more
to say about the terminus than it is usually allowed to say.

**Tunnel vision** — building all intuition on Schwarzschild and Oppenheimer–Snyder, which
are the two least generic solutions in the problem. Section 4 shows exactly how non-generic,
with a one-line algebraic condition.

The organising move is the third thing: **take the time coordinate to be arbitrary and see
which statements survive.** Statements that don't survive aren't physics; they're
bookkeeping about a foliation. That filter does most of the work here.

## 1. What 1939 and 1965 actually established

**Oppenheimer & Snyder (1939)** matched a closed dust FLRW interior to an exterior
Schwarzschild. Two results, both correct, both about *curves*:

- A comoving dust element reaches zero areal radius in **finite proper time**.
- A distant static observer never sees the surface cross `r = 2M`; the luminosity fades
  exponentially with e-folding time `3√3 M` — **25.59 μs** for a solar mass.

They flagged the zero-volume endpoint as an artefact of pressureless dust. The usual reply
is that Penrose closed that loophole, and that reply is right but is often misread.

**Penrose (1965).** If a spacetime has a non-compact Cauchy surface, satisfies the null
convergence condition `R_ab k^a k^b ≥ 0`, and contains a closed trapped surface, then it is
future null geodesically **incomplete**.

Read the conclusion carefully. It is *not* "the density becomes infinite." It is *not*
"there is a point where matter is destroyed." It is: **some inextendible causal geodesic has
finite affine length.** The theorem is silent on what, if anything, sits at the end — and
the silence is structural, because the theorem's hypotheses are global and causal while the
question "what happens there" is local and metric.

Three consequences that are routinely skipped:

1. **The singularity is not a set.** It is a property of the manifold. Every attempt to
   attach it as a boundary has known pathologies: Schmidt's b-boundary puts the singular
   point in the closure of *every* interior point for closed FRW and for Schwarzschild
   (Bosshard, Johnson) — the quotient topology is not even T₁. Geroch's g-boundary and the
   Scott–Szekeres a-boundary have their own ambiguities. Only the causal boundary
   (Geroch–Kronheimer–Penrose, repaired by Flores–Herrera–Sánchez 2011) is in decent shape,
   and it retains only causal, not metric, information.
2. **Curvature need not diverge.** Quasi-regular ("whimper") singularities are geodesically
   incomplete with bounded curvature (Ellis–Schmidt classification, 1977). Divergence is an
   extra fact about Schwarzschild, not a corollary of the theorem.
3. **Incomplete does not mean inextendible.** This is the one that breaks the standard story
   outright — see §6.

## 2. Arbitrary time: "when does the matter vanish?" has no invariant answer

Define the **survival functional** of a foliation. Let `μ` be the measure on the matter
congruence and `Σ_t` any global spacelike foliation. Set

```
N_Σ(t) = μ{ worldlines that meet Σ_t }
```

This is the formal object hiding behind every sentence of the form "the matter is gone."
It is not a diffeomorphism invariant, and the failure is not subtle:

- **Painlevé–Gullstrand / ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein.** Slices are regular across the
  horizon and run into `r = 0`. `N_Σ(t)` drops to zero at finite `t`. Matter "vanishes."
- **Maximal slicing (`K = 0`).** The lapse obeys `∇²α = α K_ij K^ij`; by the maximum
  principle `α > 0` everywhere but collapses in the strong-field region. The slices avoid
  the terminus entirely. Explicitly: maximal slices of Schwarzschild are limited by the
  double root of `f r⁴ + C²` with `f = 1 − 2M/r`, which sits at

```
      r₀ = 3M/2 ,      C = 3√3 M²/4 ,      Kretschmann there = 1024/(243 M⁴)  (finite)
```

  The foliation asymptotes to an eternal cylinder *outside* `r = 0` and never reaches it
  (Estabrook–Wahlquist–Christensen–DeWitt–Smarr–Tsiang 1973; Reinhart 1973; this is the
  "trumpet" every moving-puncture code lives on). **In this time, `N_Σ(t) = N₀` forever.
  The collapse never completes and no matter ever disappears.**

Both foliations are global, spacelike, asymptotically flat and cover the entire exterior.
They disagree about whether the matter still exists. So:

> **The proposition "matter vanishes upon reaching the end of its worldline" is a statement
> about a choice of time function, not about the spacetime.** The invariant residue is only:
> *these curves are incomplete, and here is the limiting behaviour along each.*

That residue is computable. The rest of this note computes it.

## 3. What the terminus does to a body (Schwarzschild)

Radial marginally bound infall gives `r(s) = (9M/2)^{1/3} s^{2/3}`, so the tidal
coefficient is universal:

```
      M / r³  =  2 / (9 s²)
```

Geodesic deviation in the parallel-propagated frame, `ξ̈ = −R^î_{t̂ĵt̂} ξ^ĵ`, is then an
Euler equation with exponents

```
      transverse:  p(p−1) = −2/9   →   p ∈ {1/3, 2/3}      (both shrink)
      radial:      p(p−1) = +4/9   →   p ∈ {−1/3, 4/3}     (generic mode diverges)
```

Reading the metric directly confirms it: near `r = 0` the vacuum interior is Kasner with
exponents **(−1/3, 2/3, 2/3)**, `Σp = Σp² = 1`.

So the body arrives at the terminus **stretched to unbounded length and squeezed to zero
cross-section, with 3-volume → 0.** It is not "crushed to a point." The volume vanishes,
but one dimension *blows up*. "Zero volume" and "crushed to a point" are different claims,
and only the first is true.

Two more numbers worth having. The longest proper time any worldline has between crossing
the horizon and the terminus is

```
      ∫₀^{2M} √r dr / √(2M − r)  =  π M       (15.47 μs for one solar mass)
```

so the interior is a Lorentzian metric space of **finite diameter** — a fact that matters
in §8. And in the Kasner parametrisation

```
      p₁ = −u/(1+u+u²),   p₂ = (1+u)/(1+u+u²),   p₃ = u(1+u)/(1+u+u²)
```

Schwarzschild is exactly **`u = 1`** — the locally-rotationally-symmetric point, the fixed
point of the BKL era map, the one Kasner that does not oscillate. *The canonical black hole
interior sits at the single most degenerate point of the space of terminal geometries.*

## 4. The Oppenheimer–Snyder endpoint is not merely unphysical — it is Weyl-free

O&S said dust is an idealisation. Here is a sharper and, I think, more damaging statement:
**even inside dust, their endpoint is a measure-zero degeneracy, and it degenerates in the
one direction that inverts its physical meaning.**

Take marginally bound Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi dust — same matter, same symmetry, one free
function `m(r)` instead of a constant density:

```
      Ṙ² = 2m(r)/R  ,      R(τ,r) = [ r^{3/2} − (3/2)√(2m) τ ]^{2/3}
```

Each shell crunches at its own comoving time `τ_s(r) = (2/3) r^{3/2} / √(2m)`. Setting
`s = τ_s(r) − τ` and expanding as `s → 0`:

```
      R    →  (9m/2)^{1/3} s^{2/3}

      R′   →  [ 2^{1/6} 3^{2/3} √r (3m − r m′) / (9 m^{7/6}) ] · s^{−1/3}
```

**The entire O–S special case is the vanishing of one algebraic factor: `3m − r m′ = 0`,
i.e. `m ∝ r³`, i.e. uniform density.** Off that locus — for *every other* mass profile —
the radial scale factor goes as `s^{−1/3}`, the angular ones as `s^{2/3}`, and the terminus
is the Kasner `(−1/3, 2/3, 2/3)` of §3. Generic dust spaghettifies. Uniform dust does not.
The difference is one function's worth of fine-tuning.

Now the part that changes the interpretation. Split the curvature into its Ricci (matter)
and Weyl (free gravitational field) parts. For LTB the single Weyl scalar is
`Ψ₂ = m/R³ − m′/(3R²R′)`, and the two pieces scale differently:

```
      m/R³   →   2 / (9 s²)      ← independent of m(r) entirely. Universal.
      ρ      =   m′/(4πR²R′)  ~  s^{−1}
```

Two conclusions, both from the calculation and neither optional:

**(a) The generic terminus is Weyl-dominated, and universally so.** `Ricci/Weyl = O(s) → 0`.
The collapsing matter's own energy density becomes *dynamically irrelevant* at its own
terminus. And the surviving Weyl scalar `2/(9s²)` carries no memory of `m(r)` — every dust
profile approaches the same terminal geometry.

**(b) The Oppenheimer–Snyder terminus has `Ψ₂ ≡ 0` exactly.** Its interior is FLRW, hence
conformally flat, hence Weyl-free. Verified symbolically: substituting `m = (4π/3)ρ₀r³`
gives `Ψ₂ = 0` identically, and `τ_s′(r) = 0`.

Put (a) and (b) together against Penrose's Weyl Curvature Hypothesis — that Weyl vanishes at
*initial* singularities (low entropy) and is unconstrained at *final* ones (high entropy) —
and the O–S endpoint lands on the wrong side of the ledger:

> **The Oppenheimer–Snyder endpoint has the curvature signature of a Big Bang running
> backwards, not of a generic gravitational collapse.** It is the unique Weyl-free,
> entropy-minimising member of the LTB family. Oppenheimer and Snyder were right that it is
> unphysical; the invariant reason is not that dust lacks pressure but that their endpoint
> is *time-reversed cosmology*, and it is reached only by tuning away every degree of freedom
> that makes collapse collapse.

Every intuition inherited from the 1939 picture — "matter crushed to a point of infinite
density" — is intuition about this one Weyl-free ray. The density does diverge there
(`ρ ~ s^{−2}`, matter-dominated). Nowhere else does it dominate.

## 5. So the obstruction is not a theory of matter

The standard closing line is: *we can't say what happens to the matter until we have a
theory of matter.* Section 4 undercuts the premise. At the generic terminus the matter's
stress-energy is subleading by one full power of `s`; the dynamics is the vacuum field's own
nonlinearity. Three separable questions have been fused into one:

| Question | Actually needs |
|---|---|
| **Q1** What is the terminal geometry? | Vacuum GR. Largely answered: Kasner/BKL, matter subdominant. |
| **Q2** What happens to matter's degrees of freedom? | The structure of the causal boundary — see §7, §8. Not a matter theory. |
| **Q3** Does the terminus occur, or is it replaced? | Quantum gravity. But the handover scale is set by **curvature**, `s ~ t_Planck` from `2/(9s²) ~ ℓ_Pl^{−2}` — *independent of what fell in.* |

Where matter genuinely does enter is sharper and much more interesting than "we need a
theory of it." In the BKL/cosmological-billiards picture (Damour–Henneaux–Nicolai 2003),
the terminal dynamics is a billiard in the space of Kasner exponents, and **matter enters
only by determining which walls exist** — not as an energy source:

- Pure 4D gravity → the billiard table is the Weyl chamber of the hyperbolic Kac–Moody
  algebra `AE₃`; finite volume ⇒ **chaotic**, infinitely many Kasner epochs, the `u`
  parameter iterated by the Gauss map.
- A massless scalar / stiff fluid (`p = ρ`, `ρ ~ s^{−2}` — the one matter that *isn't*
  subdominant) removes walls and **kills the chaos**: monotone Kasner (Belinski–Khalatnikov).
- D = 11 supergravity → `E₁₀`. Pure gravity in `D ≥ 11` → non-chaotic
  (Demaret–Henneaux–Spindel).

So the honest replacement for the closing line is:

> The terminus does not need a theory of matter. It needs the **field content** — the spectrum
> of p-forms and dilaton couplings that fixes which Kac–Moody Weyl chamber you are bouncing
> in. That is a far weaker and far more tractable requirement than "a theory of matter," and
> for the energy density it is not a requirement at all.

## 6. For rotating holes, the worldline may not end

Every astrophysical black hole rotates, and Kerr's interior is not Schwarzschild's.

The inner (Cauchy) horizon is destabilised by mass inflation: the Hawking mass diverges
exponentially (Poisson–Israel 1989–90; Ori 1991). Curvature blows up. But the tidal
*distortion* — the double time-integral of the tidal tensor, which is what actually deforms
a body — **converges**. Ori's weak null singularity: the metric is continuous, not `C¹`;
tidal forces diverge but their effect on an infalling body is finite.

Then Dafermos–Luk (2017) proved the `C⁰`-formulation of strong cosmic censorship **false**
for Kerr: for data converging to Kerr in the exterior, the maximal development admits a
continuous extension across the Cauchy horizon. Strong cosmic censorship is expected to
survive in the `H¹_loc` sense — the extension exists but fails to be a weak solution
(Luk–Oh, spherically symmetric Einstein–Maxwell–scalar).

Read against §1:

> Penrose guarantees an incomplete geodesic. For rotating black holes that incompleteness is
> located where the spacetime is **continuously extendible** — the worldline runs out of *this*
> solution, not out of *spacetime*. "Matter vanishes at the end of its worldline" is not
> established for any realistic black hole. What is established is that the Einstein equations
> stop determining the future, which is a failure of *predictivity*, not of *existence*.

This is the sharpest available correction to the received account, and it is not speculative:
it is a theorem, and it is about the only black holes that exist.

## 7. "Properties conferred to the hole" is a theorem about surface integrals

Why do `M`, `Q`, `J` survive while everything else does not? The usual answer invokes no-hair.
The structural answer is cleaner and does the work:

**`M`, `Q`, `J` are surface integrals at infinity** — ADM/Komar/Gauss. Their conservation is a
statement about flux through a 2-sphere in the *exterior*. It never referenced the interior,
so nothing about the terminus can threaten it. Nothing is "transferred through" anything.
The bookkeeping closes in a region the matter has already left.

Baryon number has no long-range field, hence no Gauss law, hence no exterior representative —
which is the whole content of "black holes violate baryon number." So:

> **Exactly the charges with Gauss laws are conferred. Everything else was never an exterior
> quantity to begin with.** "Conferral" is not a physical process at the singularity; it is a
> theorem about which functionals of the interior data are measurable from outside.

Formally: let `μ` be the terminal measure — the pushforward of the matter measure along the
congruence onto the causal boundary. The exterior sees only

```
      μ  ↦  (M, Q, J)     — three Gauss-protected moments of μ
```

Information loss *is* the non-injectivity of that map. The fibre over a given `(M,Q,J)` is a
space of measures on the terminal boundary, and Bekenstein–Hawking asserts its log-volume is
`A/4 = 4πM²` (`1.05 × 10⁷⁷` nats for a solar mass).

## 8. Three conjectures, each with a way to kill it

Clearly labelled: these are blackboard proposals, not results.

### C1 — Terminal Measure Program, with an entropy selection principle

Replace the failed b-boundary with the causal boundary of the maximal development
(Flores–Herrera–Sánchez), equipped with the pushforward measure `μ`, treated as a Lorentzian
metric-measure space in the synthetic framework now available (Kunzinger–Sämann Lorentzian
length spaces; Cavalletti–Mondino timelike Ricci bounds, which already yield synthetic proofs
of the Hawking and Penrose theorems). The finite Lorentzian diameter `πM` from §3 is what
makes the completion tractable.

**Selection principle (the new part):** a boundary construction should be *required* to yield
a measure space of entropy `A/4`. This is not currently demanded of any of them.

**Immediate kill-test, and I expect it to fail:** compute `μ` for Oppenheimer–Snyder, where
everything is explicit. All dust reaches the terminus simultaneously in comoving time; the
boundary is an antichain parametrised by `χ ∈ [0, χ₀]`, and its entropy is fixed by the
initial data — not `4πM²`. **The classical terminal boundary is too small to carry
Bekenstein–Hawking entropy.** If that is right, it is a clean statement of the information
problem in purely classical, purely geometric terms, and it says the entropy does not live at
the terminus.

### C2 — The Silent Terminal Boundary is an antichain

Asymptotic silence (Uggla–van Elst–Wainwright–Ellis 2003): particle horizons shrink to zero
at the terminus, spatial derivatives drop out, each spatial point decouples into its own
dynamical system.

**Conjecture:** the causal boundary of a generic terminus is an **antichain** — no two terminal
points causally related, zero Lorentzian separation, but carrying a non-trivial measure.
Then information loss is not annihilation but **causal decoupling**: each worldline's terminal
data becomes inaccessible to every other, permanently.

**Known obstruction, stated honestly:** silence fails on a measure-zero set — the "spikes" of
Uggla et al., partially controlled rigorously by Rodnianski–Speck and Fournodavlos–Rodnianski–Speck.
Whether spikes are boundary-relevant is exactly the crux. If spikes carry positive boundary
measure, C2 is dead.

### C3 — A universal classical information-destruction rate

The BKL parameter is iterated by the Gauss map `u ↦ 1/(u−1)`, whose invariant measure is
`du/((1+u)ln 2)` and whose Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy is

```
      h = π² / (6 ln 2)  =  2.3731 nats  =  3.4237 bits per BKL era
```

**Conjecture:** this is the *rate* of classical information destruction at a chaotic terminus —
per spatial cell, per era — and it is **universal**, independent of what collapsed. Its
universality is the same kind of universality as `A/4`.

**Test:** count effective silent cells inside the horizon in the asymptotic regime, multiply
by `h` per era, cut off at the Planck era. If that reproduces `4πM²`, the coincidence is not
one. If it misses by many orders of magnitude — the likely outcome — then C3 is a statement
about the classical cascade only, and combined with C1's expected failure it says the entropy
lives on the horizon and not at the terminus, which is itself worth establishing.

## 8b. Two proofs, added after the numeric companion found them

Everything above this line was either cited (a theorem I did not prove) or checked
numerically (an exponent, a ratio, a coincidence to some number of decimal places).
Building `terminus.py` turned up two claims that are neither — they admit short,
complete, symbolically-verified proofs. Both are stated precisely, so a wrong step
would be falsifiable; neither is claimed as unknown to specialists in this field,
only as more than I had actually shown before checking.

### Theorem 1 — the BKL "chord" is exact in the Bianchi II limit, not approximate

`terminus.py`'s §9 measured the trajectory of a real Mixmaster bounce in the
`(Σ₊,Σ₋)` anisotropy plane against a straight line joining its flanking Kasner
points, and found agreement to within `10⁻⁴` to `10⁻⁷` depending on the bounce —
good, but unexplained, and reported as an empirical fact with no claim about why.

Restrict to the idealized limit the heuristic actually describes: Bianchi II
vacuum, meaning `N₂ = N₃ = 0` identically and `Ω = 0` (no matter). The vacuum
constraint forces `q = 2 − (3/2)N₁²`, and substituting collapses the `S₀` equations
of motion to

```
      dΣ₊/dτ = (3/2) N₁² (2 − Σ₊)
      dΣ₋/dτ = −(3/2) N₁² Σ₋
```

Divide one by the other: **`N₁²` cancels exactly**, leaving a first-order ODE in the
plane alone, with no `τ`- or `N₁`-dependence left at all:

```
      dΣ₋/dΣ₊  =  −Σ₋ / (2 − Σ₊)
```

This is separable: `d(ln Σ₋) = d(ln(Σ₊ − 2))`, so

> **`K := Σ₋/(Σ₊ − 2)` is an exact constant of motion of the Bianchi II vacuum flow.**

Not to leading order — exactly. Differentiating `K` along the actual flow using the
two boxed equations above, with no small-`N₁` truncation anywhere, gives
`dK/dτ ≡ 0` as a rational-function identity (`verify.py`, THEOREM 1). Every Bianchi
II bounce therefore lies **exactly** on a straight chord of the Kasner circle — and
every such chord, extended, passes through the single point `(Σ₊,Σ₋) = (2,0)`, which
sits outside the physical disk (`4 > 1`): the whole one-parameter family of bounces
is a pencil of secants through a common external point.

`terminus.py` §10 confirms this by integrating the *reduced* 3-variable system
directly — so `N₂ = N₃ = 0` holds by construction, not by falling below a detection
threshold — across five initial conditions, through a full nonlinear bounce
(`N₁` sweeping from `~10⁻²⁰⁰` to `O(1)` and back). `K` is conserved to
**`10⁻¹⁵`–`10⁻¹⁸`**: the floor of double-precision arithmetic, not a small residual.

That also retroactively explains §9's `10⁻⁴`–`10⁻⁷`: it is not a physical correction
from the two "quiescent" axes being merely small rather than zero. Checking directly,
their squared size during a bounce runs `~10⁻³⁰` to `~10⁻¹⁷⁰` — many, many orders of
magnitude below the observed residual, so that tidy story is **false**, and I would
have been wrong to assert it without checking. The residual is dominated by
something cruder: §9 selects each chord's endpoint operationally, as whichever
sample first drops below a fixed threshold `|N_i| < 3×10⁻²`, which is an `O(threshold)`
choice about *where in the shell to sample*, not a feature of the dynamics. Swap the
operational proxy for the real reduced system and the residual doesn't shrink — it
disappears to machine precision.

### Theorem 2 — the maximal-slicing radius `r₀ = 3M/2` is a true extremum, by hand

Section 2's limiting cylinder came from asking `sympy` to solve
`P(r,C) = r⁴ − 2Mr³ + C² = 0` jointly with `∂P/∂r = 0`. That's solid, but it's a
computer asserting a fact, not a proof of one. The fact itself is elementary:

> **Claim.** `h(r) := r³(2M − r)` attains its maximum on `(0, 2M)` uniquely at
> `r = 3M/2`, where `h = 27M⁴/16`.

*Proof.* `h(0) = h(2M) = 0`, and `h(r) > 0` on the open interval, so the maximum is
interior. `h'(r) = 6Mr² − 4r³ = 2r²(3M − 2r)`, whose only zero on `r > 0` is
`r = 3M/2` (the factor `r²` contributes only the boundary point `r=0`, excluded since
`r>0`). `h''(3M/2) = −9M² < 0`, so it is a genuine maximum, and since it is the only
interior critical point it is the global one. Direct substitution gives
`h(3M/2) = (3M/2)³(M/2) = 27M⁴/16`. ∎

Since `P(r,C) = 0 ⟺ C² = h(r)`, this is exactly the statement that `C² = 27M⁴/16` is
the largest value of `C²` for which the maximal-slicing radial profile has *any* real
solution at all — the algebraic content of why the family of slices cannot be pushed
past `r₀ = 3M/2`. (The physical embedding — that boundary-time evolution actually
drives the family toward this extremal `C`, asymptotically, never reaching it — is
Estabrook–Wahlquist–Christensen–DeWitt–Smarr–Tsiang 1973 and Reinhart 1973, cited
not re-derived; what's new here is only the hand proof of the extremal-value fact
itself, in place of "a computer algebra system solved it.")

## 9. What I would retract first

In descending order of fragility:

1. **C3** is the most speculative thing here. The mapping from "BKL eras" to "bits per unit
   proper time" needs a regulator I have not constructed, and the cell count is hand-waved.
2. **C1's entropy selection principle** is a proposal about what a definition *ought* to
   satisfy. Reasonable people can reject it and keep the causal boundary as-is.
3. **C2** dies immediately if spikes are shown to carry boundary measure.
4. **§5's inversion of the premise** is safe for dust and for any matter with `p < ρ`, and
   *fails for stiff matter* (`p = ρ`), where `ρ ~ s^{−2}` is not subdominant. Stated as a
   universal claim it would be wrong; stated with the equation-of-state condition it holds.
5. **§4's derivation** is a two-line expansion in a textbook solution, symbolically
   verified, and I would defend it as written.
6. **§6** is not mine and is not fragile. It is Dafermos–Luk, and it is a theorem.
7. **§8b's Theorem 2** is elementary calculus with no moving parts — the only way it's
   wrong is arithmetic, and `verify.py` rules that out.
8. **§8b's Theorem 1** is the sturdiest thing in the note. It is checked two
   independent ways — a symbolic identity (`dK/dτ ≡ 0`, exactly, not approximately)
   and a numeric integration of the actual nonlinear system to `10⁻¹⁵`–`10⁻¹⁸` — and
   the two agree. If I had to bet on exactly one claim here surviving scrutiny
   unchanged, this is the one.

## 10. The blackboard in one paragraph

The received story runs: matter falls in, its worldline ends, its properties pass to the hole,
and we cannot say more without a theory of matter. Each clause weakens under the arbitrary-time
filter. *"Its worldline ends"* is Penrose's incompleteness, which for rotating holes sits at a
continuously extendible boundary — the equations stop predicting, the spacetime does not stop.
*"Matter vanishes"* is foliation-dependent; in maximal slicing nothing ever vanishes and the
collapse never completes. *"Properties conferred"* is a theorem about Gauss laws in the
exterior, not a process at the terminus. And *"we need a theory of matter"* has it backwards:
at the generic terminus the matter's energy density is subleading by a full power of `s`, the
geometry is Weyl-dominated and profile-independent, and matter enters only by choosing which
Kac–Moody Weyl chamber the billiard bounces in. The 1939 endpoint that started all of this is
not merely unphysical — it is the unique Weyl-free, entropy-minimising ray in its own family,
which is to say it is a Big Bang played backwards. The generic terminus looks nothing like it,
and most of what remains unknown there is a question about the gravitational field, not about
what fell in.
